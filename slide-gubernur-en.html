This is not a new approach. A near-identical individual-based model, built by the University of Glasgow team in 2009–2013, directly informed the campaign that cut Bali's human rabies deaths by over 90% and reached 11 case-free months by mid-2013. We are proposing to resume it, updated with thirteen further years of confirmed data.

Why A₀ and not the textbook R₀: the University of Glasgow's Bali model estimated a per-transmission-generation R₀≈1.2. Because rabies has a multi-week generation time, several generations compound within one calendar year — so this deck's annual growth factor (A₀=2.96) is the empirically observed yearly result of that lower per-generation rate, not a contradiction of it.

First-phase investment priority

Before scaling: validate dog population data through the RaCon census

Priority through December 2026. No valid, reliable dog population data exists for Bali today — so vaccination coverage and every other program decision in the preceding slides still rests on estimates, not a census. RaCon is proposed as the single official data source for every rabies programme in Bali.

Already underway: a population census has been carried out by the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine (FKH), Udayana University, in pilot villages, integrated with Tri Dharma (the three pillars of Indonesian higher education) coursework — the recommended approach to expand across Bali via annual KKN Tematik (thematic community service), involving every university in Bali. Students will also teach communities how to use RaCon and deliver rabies health promotion, for sustainable community engagement.
